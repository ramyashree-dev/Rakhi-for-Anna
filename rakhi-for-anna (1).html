The Letter

Dear Anna,

I keep thinking about how far back "us" actually goes. Before I understood what having a brother even meant, you were already there — in the background of almost every memory I have from being little.

We grew up together, and somewhere in between all the playing and the learning, we also fought. A lot, honestly. Over the smallest, silliest things. You annoyed me on purpose more times than I can count, and I probably gave it right back. If anyone ever asks what sibling love looks like, I think it looks a little bit like that — two people who drove each other crazy and somehow never actually meant it.

What I don't say often enough is that underneath all the teasing, you've always had this quietly caring side. You check on me without making it a whole thing. You warn me before I do something silly. You help before I even ask. You've stood beside me more times than you probably remember, and I remember more of them than you'd think.

I've also gotten to watch you grow — from the loud, mischievous kid you were, into someone I genuinely look up to in your own way. You've changed, you've learned, you've made mistakes and figured things out, and I've been lucky enough to see all of it up close.

I don't always say "I love you" or talk about my feelings the way maybe I should. But even when we fight, even when we're annoyed at each other, even on the days we barely talk — none of that ever actually breaks what we have. It just doesn't.

So thank you — for being my brother, my protector, my personal irritator, my unofficial advisor, and one of the very few people I know I can always count on. We've grown up together, and somehow all those ordinary, everyday moments turned into some of the most important parts of my life.

No matter how old we get, no matter where life takes us, I'll always be your little sister. And you'll always be my brother. Always.

Happy Raksha Bandhan, Anna. 🪢❤️

— Your little sister, Ramya 🫶
